Every screen, every button, and what happens when you press it. Written for the people doing the hiring, not the people who set the tool up.

Four screens Two ways to work Nothing sends without a human
START

The idea in one minute

Your candidates live in a Google Sheet. This dashboard reads that sheet, helps you write to people, and writes the result back. There is nothing else to learn: if a name is in the sheet, it is in the app, and every change the app makes appears in the sheet.

There are two ways to work, and knowing which one you are in explains most of the interface:

WayWhat it is forWhere
The pipeline Forty applicants for one role, all getting the same kind of email. Tick them, generate drafts, read, approve, send in one go. Top toolbar of the Inbox
The composer One person, one message. Say what it should cover in plain English; the model writes it using their details from the sheet. Right-hand panel of the Inbox

Both end in the same place — a real email from a real mailbox — and both stop for a human before they get there.

The four screens

SCREEN 1

Inbox

Everyone from the Applicants tab, newest activity first.

The banner at the top

Read this before anything else. It has three states and it tells you whether your clicks will reach real people.

BannerMeaning
Dry run is onNothing leaves the building. Sends are recorded in the log and marked dry run. Safe to experiment.
Live sending is enabledEmails reach real candidates. The Send buttons turn red as a second reminder.
Sending is brokenDry run is off but no mailbox is configured. Every send is refused — nothing is quietly recorded as sent. Tell whoever set the app up.

The six counters

Total · Awaiting draft · Awaiting approval · Ready to send · Sent · Needs attention.

Needs attention is the one to watch: it combines rows that failed with duplicate rows. Anything above zero deserves a look before a bulk send.

The pipeline toolbar

These four act on everyone you have ticked in the list. They are deliberately greyed out unless every ticked row is at the right stage — you cannot approve something that was never drafted.

The Send button reads Dry-run send while dry run is on, and turns red reading Send when it is off. The confirmation lists the first twelve recipients so you can see who is about to be emailed.

Refresh from sheet

The sheet can be edited by anyone at any time — by hand, by a paste, by a colleague — so the page can go stale while you look at it. ⟳ Refresh from sheet re-reads it without losing your place, your selection, or your scroll position. The label beside it says how long ago the data was read.

Refresh after anyone pastes new rows in, and before any bulk send.

The duplicates warning

If two rows share an applicant_id or an email address, a warning appears naming the exact sheet rows, and every affected row gets a duplicate tag in the list.

A repeated id is the dangerous one. Actions find a person by id and take the first row that matches, so acting on the second one silently changes the first. You would see a success message naming the right person while the wrong row moved. A repeated email is milder — that person simply gets the email twice. Fix both in the sheet, then press Refresh.

Finding people

  • Search covers name, email, id, role and notes.
  • Three filters — role, stage, category — combine with each other and with the search.
  • Group by stacks the list under headings by category, stage or role. Useful for seeing an entire role's pipeline at once.
  • Bulk category: tick several people and a box appears to set the category on all of them.

Adding someone

+ New adds a row to the Applicants tab without opening the spreadsheet. Only the email is required; role and category offer suggestions from what already exists.

Fill in Notes if you can. It is passed to the model verbatim and it is the difference between a generic email and one that mentions where you met them.

The candidate panel

Select someone and the right-hand side fills in: their name, address, stage, category, role and id, then their notes in italics, then the last email that was sent to them.

Two fields are editable right there, each with its own Save:

  • Email — for fixing a typo or filling in a blank. A send to a malformed address is refused, so this saves a trip to the spreadsheet. The app will not let you save an address that already belongs to someone else.
  • Category — affects which template matches them.

Each Save button stays greyed out until you actually change the value.

SCREEN 1B

Writing to one person

The composer, in the order you use it.

1 · Say what the email should cover

Plain English, in the big box. You are briefing a colleague, not writing the email:

invite her to a 30-minute intro call next week, mention it is
remote, ask for two possible time slots

You never type their name, role or your company details. Those come from their sheet row and the branding settings. Being specific about the content is what improves the result — being specific about the person is already done for you.

2 · Base it on a template (optional)

Only active templates appear here. Choosing one gives the model a structure and tone to follow. Leaving it on No template lets the model write from your brief alone.

3 · Fill the boxes

ButtonWhat it doesNeeds
✨ Write with AIWrites a fresh email from your brief, their details and the template's tone.A brief, or a template
Use template as-isFills the template in with their details. No model involved, instant, free.A template

Both only ever fill the boxes below. Nothing is sent, and nothing is saved to the sheet, until you press Send yourself. Run ✨ Write with AI as many times as you like; each press replaces what is there.

4 · Read it and edit it

Subject and Message are ordinary text boxes — edit freely. The message is HTML, so <p> makes a paragraph and <br> a line break, but you can also just type sentences. Preview underneath shows it as the recipient will see it.

Always read it before sending. The model is fast and usually good, and neither of those is the same as correct. It is writing to a real person about their job.

5 · Attach files (optional)

📎 Attach files takes files from your computer for this one email. Several at a time, up to 15MB total; each appears as a removable chip. For a file that should go out with every use of a template, set it on the template instead — see the next screen.

6 · Send

The button reads Send, or a red Send for real when dry run is off. It stays greyed out until the subject and message are both filled and no {{field}} remains.

If you see "Still has an unfilled {{field}}", some placeholder never got a value — often because that person's row is missing a detail. Type over it, or fill the cell in the sheet and refresh. The app blocks the send rather than mail somebody a literal {{name}}.

SCREEN 2

Templates

Reusable emails. This is where the house tone lives.

Generating one

Describe the email — purpose, tone, optionally a role and extra instructions — and press Generate.

Generated templates arrive switched off. They are saved inactive and cannot be used until you preview one and press Activate. Nothing a model wrote can reach a candidate before a person has read it.

Reading the table

ColumnMeaning
ScopeWhich role and category it is for. default marks the fallback used when nothing else fits.
AIpersonalised means it contains {{ai_body}}, so each use writes a fresh paragraph for that person and spends model quota. static means the same words every time, and costs nothing.
FileAn attachment that goes out with every send. Click it to check the link.
Statusactive templates can be used; inactive ones are invisible everywhere else in the app.

Preview, activate, attach

Preview opens the template rendered as an email, with its raw HTML underneath if you want it. From there you can also set an attachment.

Attachments are a public link, not an upload — a Google Drive file shared "anyone with the link", or any other public URL. It is fetched fresh at send time, so replacing the file at that link updates every future email with no template edit. Nothing over 15MB will send.

How a template gets chosen

When you press Generate drafts in bulk, the app scores every active template against each person:

SituationEffect
Template's role matches theirsStrongly preferred
Template is for a different roleDisqualified — never used by mistake
Category also matchesPreferred over role alone
Nothing fitsFalls back to the default, and records that it did

So: one default template as a safety net, plus a specific one per role. Leave a template's role blank to let it apply to anybody.

SCREEN 3

Settings

Three switches that matter, and the values every email is built from.

Dry run

The banner at the top, with a button reading Go live or Back to dry run. Turning it off asks for confirmation, because it is the single switch between practising and emailing real people.

Leave it on while learning, while testing a new template, and any time you are unsure. Sends are still logged, so a dry run tells you exactly what would have happened.

The two master switches

SwitchControls
DraftingThe Generate drafts action. Off means no model quota can be spent in bulk.
SendingEvery send, bulk and one-off. Off by default — turn it on deliberately.

These take effect on the next click, with no restart. They are also enforced behind the scenes, so switching one off genuinely stops the action rather than only hiding the button.

Other settings

Shown read-only, edited in the Config tab of the spreadsheet. These are the values every email is built from — company name, the signature, the phone numbers, the reply-to address, the daily cap.

Change one and every future email changes with it. Nothing already sent is affected.

SCREEN 4

Console

Where you go when something looks wrong.

Preflight

Run preflight checks every credential without sending anything or writing a row: the sheet is reachable, its columns are right, the model answers, the mailbox logs in, and there are no duplicate ids or malformed addresses.

Run it after any change to the spreadsheet's structure, and before a big send. If a check fails it names the fix.

The email log

Every send attempt ever made, newest first, with three counters above it: delivered today, failed today, logged all time.

ResultMeaning
sentGenuinely delivered to the mail server.
dry runSimulated. Nothing was delivered.
failedRejected. Hover the tag for the reason.

The log is written before the candidate's row is updated, so a send can never be missing from it. It is the honest record — if the two ever disagree, believe the log.

Every real send also lands in the sending account's Gmail Sent folder, which is a second, independent copy.

Stages

The tag on every candidate. It is the app's memory of where you left off.

The normal path NEW→DRAFTED→APPROVED→SENT→REPLIED→CLOSED When something goes wrong FAILED→ back to any earlier stage, so a retry is always possible
StageMeansSet by
NEWIn the sheet, nothing written yet.You, adding them
DRAFTEDAn email exists and is waiting to be read.Generate drafts
APPROVEDA human read it and it is fit to send.Approve
SENTIt left the building.Send
REPLIEDThey answered. Set by hand — replies arrive in a real mailbox, not here.You, in the sheet
CLOSEDFinished with. The end of the line.You, in the sheet

A failed send never claims to have worked. The row stays APPROVED with the error recorded, so you can fix the cause and press Send again without wondering whether it went out twice.

Three recipes

Forty applicants for one role

  1. Paste them into the Applicants tab, then press ⟳ Refresh from sheet.
  2. Clear the duplicates warning if one appears.
  3. Filter to that role. Tick the ones you want.
  4. Generate drafts.
  5. Open several and read them. Not all forty — enough to trust the template.
  6. Tick, Approve, then Send. Check the addresses in the confirmation.
  7. Check the Console log afterwards.

One message to one person

  1. Search their name, select them.
  2. Check the email address is right; fix it there if not.
  3. Type the brief. Pick a template if you want its tone.
  4. ✨ Write with AI, read, edit.
  5. Attach anything needed, then Send.

Someone new, not from the sheet

  1. + New, fill in at least the email, and write a real note about them.
  2. They are selected automatically — write to them straight away.

What the app will not let you do

Worth knowing, because it tells you how much you can safely experiment.

  • Nothing sends by itself. There is no schedule and no background job. Every email is a button a person pressed.
  • The AI never sends. It only fills boxes you then read.
  • Generated templates start inactive. A person has to activate them.
  • Sending is off twice over by default — the dry run switch and the sending switch, both flipped deliberately.
  • An unfilled {{field}} blocks the send rather than mailing someone a placeholder.
  • A broken mailbox refuses sends rather than logging them as delivered.
  • A failed send leaves the row unsent, so retrying is always safe.
  • Bulk sends confirm first, listing who is about to be emailed.
